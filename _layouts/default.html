Etienne Meunier

I am a Postdoc at Inria Ange Team in Paris, where I work closely with LOCEAN-IPSL on machine learning methods for physical systems, with a focus on ocean and climate modelling. I came to climate science after a PhD on unsupervised training of neural networks for motion analysis, which I did in the Serpico team supervised by Patrick Bouthemy. Before that, from 2019 to 2020, I was a visiting scholar in the Mostafavi Lab at the University of British Columbia, where I developed deep learning approaches to interpret genomics data.

My interests lie where machine learning meets physics. I like building models that learn from data while still respecting the equations behind it, whether they are hybrid models that stay stable over long simulations, generative models that produce physically consistent ocean states, or differentiable simulators. Along the way, I enjoy working with climate scientists and writing research code that others can actually use.

At Inria / IPSL, I co-supervise the PhDs of Blandine Gorce, who builds generative models to find stable states of large-scale partial differential equations, and Maya Janvier, who works on making hybrid models stable. I also work closely with Rares Dimitrie Grozavescu at the University of Cambridge on Koopman operators, and with Markus Jochum at the Niels Bohr Institute in Copenhagen on differentiable ocean models.

If you work on related topics, or you are a student looking for an internship, feel free to reach out!

I also have a Blog where I write about the things I am interested in and wish I had more time to work on!

Research

My current work focuses on machine learning for physical systems and ocean modelling. Before that, I worked on unsupervised video analysis. Today I mainly work on three things:

  • Hybrid models. Coupling neural networks with numerical models, and making sure they stay stable over long simulations.
  • Generative models for the ocean. Producing physically consistent ocean states, for example to speed up the spin-up of ocean models.
  • Differentiable and learned dynamics. Differentiable ocean models and learned latent dynamics such as Koopman operators, for forecasting and data assimilation.
